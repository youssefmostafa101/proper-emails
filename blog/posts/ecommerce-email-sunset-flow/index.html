Ecommerce Email Sunset Flow: How to Clean Your List Without Losing Valuable Customers

Give persistently inactive subscribers a respectful last chance to engage, then suppress appropriately—with a practical Klaviyo setup, example emails, and QA checklist.

Not every subscriber who stops opening your emails has stopped caring about your brand. Some people are between purchases, some have changed addresses, and others simply no longer want marketing messages. If you keep sending campaigns to everyone indefinitely, however, your program can accumulate an audience that rarely interacts.

The answer is not to delete anyone who missed the last few campaigns. It is to build a thoughtful ecommerce email sunset flow that gives selected inactive subscribers a final chance to remain engaged, then stops routine marketing where appropriate.

Here is how sunset flows work, how to identify the right audience, and how to implement them in Klaviyo without confusing a profile tag with actual suppression.

What is an ecommerce email sunset flow?

An ecommerce email sunset flow is an automated sequence designed to phase persistently unengaged subscribers out of a brand's routine email marketing. Its purpose is to answer a practical question: should we continue sending marketing emails to this person?

A typical sunset process identifies profiles with sustained inactivity, sends a limited final re-engagement sequence, gives people a clear opportunity to remain subscribed or leave, then reviews candidates for suppression.

The goal is not to persuade every inactive subscriber to make an immediate purchase. It is to make a sensible distinction between people who may still want to hear from your brand and those who are better left alone.

Sunset flow vs. winback flow

A winback flow is generally triggered by lapsing purchasing activity; its job is to help a previous buyer consider a relevant return. A sunset flow focuses on whether an email profile should continue receiving marketing. A repeat customer who rarely clicks promotional emails and a long-term subscriber who never purchased are not in the same state. Evaluate purchase context separately from marketing engagement before making suppression decisions.

Why ecommerce brands need sunset flows

An email address does not remain relevant indefinitely just because it once subscribed. Customer interests change; products are purchased at different intervals, and messages that used to be useful may no longer be welcome.

Reduce unnecessary sending

Continuously sending routine campaigns to profiles with long-term inactivity creates volume without a clear purpose. A documented sunset process gives your team an alternative to sending indefinitely.

Protect sending relevance and deliverability

Repeatedly contacting recipients who consistently show no activity can contribute to deliverability problems. Klaviyo recommends identifying and suppressing genuinely inactive profiles as part of list hygiene. This is not a guarantee of inbox placement: authentication, complaints, acquisition practices and sending behavior also matter. 1 4

Improve the meaning of your reporting

A campaign sent to a very large, inactive audience may be difficult to compare with a campaign sent to established engaged customers. Clarifying who is eligible to receive email helps you interpret performance, although it does not automatically create incremental sales.

Respect subscribers' preferences

A responsible sunset email makes it easy to stay, update preferences or leave. It should not pressure people with invented scarcity or pretend an expiring offer exists when it does not.

How to identify inactive email subscribers

The first mistake is often a universal rule such as “suppress anyone who has not opened an email for 30 days.” That can be too blunt for products with different purchase cycles and for subscribers whose recorded opens are affected by tracking behavior.

Review email activity

Inspect recorded opens, meaningful clicks, delivered messages and unsubscribes. Apple Mail Privacy Protection can generate recorded opens without confirming human readership, so an open is not reliable proof of attention. Where available, use clicks and business events as additional evidence.

Review store and product activity

Properly tracked product views, site activity, checkout starts and completed orders may reveal continuing interest even when marketing engagement is weak. Verify these events exist and are being passed accurately before relying on them.

Review purchase history and product cadence

A customer buying an expensive, infrequently replaced product should not automatically be treated like a subscriber to a monthly consumables store. Consider order recency and the natural cycle of the category.

Review profile age and send history

Someone who joined recently and received a single campaign has not had the same chance to engage as a profile that has received many messages over months. Your definition needs enough history to be meaningful.

How Klaviyo defines a sunset segment

As documented by Klaviyo in its March 2026 sunset-flow guidance, the prebuilt Sunset (Email) segment uses a deliberately restrictive combination of conditions. A profile must be eligible for email marketing, created at least 180 days ago, and have received at least five emails in the last 72 weeks. It also has no recorded opens, clicks, Active on Site, Viewed Product, Checkout Started or Placed Order activity over the specified all-time history. These are joined with AND, not OR. 1

This is a platform example, not a universal inactivity definition. A DTC brand with longer purchase cycles, incomplete tracking or multiple email integrations may need to review the scope before using it. The source also distinguishes this prebuilt segment from other potential inactive-contact segments.

Operator check: Before launching, inspect a sample of eligible profiles. Confirm marketing consent, order history, recent tracked activity and whether your integration has actually recorded the events used in the segment.

A segment that looks clean in the editor can still be incorrect if its connectors or event names are wrong. Klaviyo specifically notes that confusing AND and OR conditions can prevent the intended audience from qualifying. 2

How to build a sunset flow in Klaviyo

The steps below follow Klaviyo's published workflow, with an additional operational review before suppression. The interface labels can change, so confirm them in your actual account. 1

Step 1: Create or review the Sunset (Email) segment

Navigate to Audience → Lists & segments. Use Klaviyo's Segment Library or build a reviewed custom segment, documenting each qualifying condition. Check that subscribers can receive email marketing and that inactivity signals reflect the business's actual customer journey.

Step 2: Create a segment-triggered flow

Go to Flows → Create flow, search for the prebuilt sunset flow, and select the sunset segment as its trigger. Alternatively, build a custom flow with an Added to segment trigger.

Existing members need special attention: In segment-triggered flows, profiles who already qualify when you create the segment may not automatically enter. Klaviyo documents an Add past profiles option for this situation; review eligibility and sending risk before using it. 3

Step 3: Add relevant engagement filters

Klaviyo's documented example uses Opened Email zero times since starting this flow and Clicked Email zero times since starting this flow. These filters let profiles who show recorded engagement skip later messages and the final tagging step.

Because opens can be inflated by privacy features, confirm whether additional customer events or a more conservative manual review should influence suppression. Do not assume a tracking event proves actual interest or lack of it.

Step 4: Write one to three focused emails

Klaviyo recommends limiting a sunset sequence to no more than three emails. An unnecessarily long series can undermine the purpose. Each message should be genuinely useful, with prominent preference-management and unsubscribe options that actually work.

Step 5: Allow a final response period

After the last email, leave a short grace period before marking profiles for review. Klaviyo illustrates three days for frequent senders and suggests a longer period, such as seven to ten days, where sending is less frequent. These are examples, not required universal schedules.

Step 6: Tag unresolved profiles

Use a Property update action to set a Boolean custom property such as Unengaged = True for profiles that reach the end without qualifying engagement.

Critical distinction: Updating Unengaged = True does not unsubscribe or suppress the profile. Klaviyo requires a separate suppression action, or a separately built and approved API workflow. 1

Step 7: Review and suppress the right profiles

Create a segment for profiles marked Unengaged = True. Before suppression, recheck recent clicks, orders and other meaningful activity. Then use the appropriate suppression workflow on the segment's current members.

Bulk suppression applies to the segment members at that moment: people joining later are not automatically suppressed. Klaviyo's documentation explains that the native flow does not itself perform email suppression, so teams need a repeatable operational process or a carefully reviewed implementation. 4

A practical two-email sunset sequence

Consider a hypothetical DTC homeware brand. The following examples illustrate a respectful tone, not proven copy or a guarantee that two messages are right for every account.

Email 1: Give subscribers a useful choice

Subject: Still interested in hearing from us?

Preview: Tell us what you would like to receive.

Headline: Better emails, on your terms.

We've shared a few updates since you joined our list, but we know not every email will be relevant. If you're still interested in new collections, product care tips or occasional news, we'd like to keep sharing useful information. If your interests have changed, that's fine too.

Primary CTA: Update your preferences

This CTA must lead to a genuine preference-management page. Include a functioning unsubscribe link, and don't offer preferences the store has not configured.

Email 2: A respectful final check-in

Subject: Should we stop sending these updates?

Preview: One last check before we reduce our emails.

Headline: We'll leave the decision with you.

We only want to send emails that are useful to you. If you'd like to keep receiving updates, you can confirm your preferences below. Otherwise, we'll stop including you in our regular marketing emails according to our list-management process.

Primary CTA: Manage email preferences

Keep the language precise: the real sending and suppression policy must match what the email says. Avoid false urgency, fabricated rewards or unapproved discounts.

What happens after the sunset flow?

A message sequence is not a complete sunset process. Someone needs to decide which profiles remain eligible and how those decisions affect future sends.

Profile behaviorSuggested next action
Meaningful engagement recordedReview and retain eligibility where appropriate
Recent purchase recordedReassess the customer lifecycle before suppression
Preferences updatedHonor the latest preferences
Unsubscribe requestedStop marketing according to applicable requirements
Still inactive after sequenceReview for suppression
Tracking or consent is unclearInvestigate before a consequential action

Automation can identify candidates. Your suppression policy should still account for data completeness, customer relevance and consent.

Sunset flow vs. re-engagement campaign: which should you use?

If your list contains a one-time group of older subscribers, a carefully scoped re-engagement campaign may be appropriate. If inactive profiles continually enter a clearly defined segment, a segment-triggered sunset flow can provide a repeatable process.

Klaviyo describes both options for older lists, and recommends caution with audiences that have not been contacted for a long time. Sending a large burst to dormant addresses can create unnecessary deliverability risk. 3

Consider list age, consent history, sending frequency, event tracking and the team's ability to review suppression. Decide which method fits the actual account—not the most convenient automation template.

How to measure whether a sunset flow is working

A sunset flow is partly a subscriber-management process, so attributed revenue alone does not capture its purpose. Review four types of evidence:

1. Audience eligibility

How many profiles qualified? Were they correctly classified? Did recent customers or active browsers appear in the segment when they should not?

2. Flow operation

Did intended profiles enter? Why were messages skipped? Were the final delays and property updates executed as configured?

3. Subscriber decisions

How many profiles clicked, changed preferences, unsubscribed or made a qualifying purchase? Were responsive profiles removed from the suppression candidate group?

4. Deliverability and audience quality

Observe bounce and complaint trends, relevant click measures and sending volume among the remaining active population. Interpret them in context: if the campaign strategy also changed, you cannot attribute every trend to the sunset flow.

For a broader measurement framework, see our guide to ecommerce email marketing metrics. Attribution is not equivalent to incremental impact.

Common sunset-flow mistakes to avoid

Using a universal inactivity threshold

A single period cannot represent every purchase cycle. Combine appropriate email and customer signals.

Sending too many re-engagement messages

A sunset flow is a final effort, not an unlimited promotional sequence. Keep it short and avoid pressuring recipients.

Depending only on open rates

Privacy mechanisms can make opens unreliable as evidence of actual human attention. Check broader signals.

Suppressing profiles without rechecking activity

Profiles may engage or buy after entering the sunset segment. Review the most recent information before suppressing.

Assuming a profile tag equals suppression

Unengaged = True simply marks the profile. Suppression remains a separate step.

Ignoring consent and channel differences

Email suppression does not automatically mean the same action occurs for SMS or push messages. Use the correct channel-specific status and applicable consent controls.

Promising a preference center that does not exist

Don't ask subscribers to update preferences unless the link actually works and the options are available.

Frequently asked questions about sunset flows

What is a sunset flow in email marketing?

A limited series for persistently inactive subscribers, followed by a controlled process to stop regular marketing to profiles that remain unengaged.

How many emails should a sunset flow contain?

Klaviyo recommends no more than three emails. The appropriate count depends on audience history and sending risk.

Should I suppress everyone who has not opened in 90 days?

Not automatically. Review actual purchases, customer activity, email history, privacy-related opens and product replacement cycles.

Can sunset flows improve deliverability?

Suppressing persistently inactive profiles can support list hygiene, but is not a guaranteed solution for deliverability problems.

Does Klaviyo automatically suppress profiles at the end?

Not when using the documented property-update action. Tagging Unengaged = True requires a separate suppression step.

Is a sunset flow the same as a winback flow?

No. Winback addresses lapsed purchase behavior; sunset addresses whether to continue sending marketing emails.

Can suppressed subscribers still receive transactional messages?

Marketing suppression and essential transactional communications are separate concepts, but actual eligibility depends on the platform configuration and relevant legal requirements. Do not send promotional content under a transactional label.

Final takeaway: retention also means knowing when to stop

A useful ecommerce email system does not try to keep every subscriber on every list indefinitely. Some customers need education, some need post-purchase help, some need a thoughtful re-engagement message, and some should no longer receive routine marketing.

A sunset flow makes that last decision more deliberate. Start with reliable segments, keep the sequence limited, verify the filters, review recent activity, and suppress carefully.

The aim is not the largest possible list. It is an email audience your brand can communicate with responsibly and usefully.
