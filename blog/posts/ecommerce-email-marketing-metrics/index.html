Ecommerce Email Marketing Metrics: What to Track Beyond Open Rates

Go beyond open rates to understand clicks, customer actions, attributed revenue, deliverability and the decisions your next report should support.

Your email campaign reports a strong open rate. The dashboard looks healthy, the subject line seems to have worked, and everyone is happy. But there is a better question: what happened after the email was delivered?

Did people click through to your store, consider products and place orders? Were those orders merely credited to the email, or is there evidence the campaign created additional business?

For ecommerce brands, reporting should support decisions rather than supply impressive numbers for a presentation. That requires knowing what the metrics actually measure and where their limitations are. This guide explains how to review campaigns and automated flows without over-reading opens or confusing attribution with incremental revenue.

What are ecommerce email marketing metrics?

Ecommerce email marketing metrics are measurements used to evaluate email delivery, recipient engagement, customer actions and business outcomes. They help operators answer four different questions:

  • Delivery: Did receiving mail servers accept the messages?
  • Engagement: Did recipients interact with the emails?
  • Customer action: Did people complete relevant actions afterwards?
  • Business outcome: What happened to orders and the longer customer relationship?

No single metric answers all four. Click rate can indicate interest in content or a destination, but not whether the business earned incremental profit. Revenue credited to an email is useful reporting context, but it is not automatic evidence that email caused every purchase.

Start by defining the question your report should answer, then choose measurements that are appropriate to that question.

Why open rates should not be your main success metric

Open rate is familiar because it is easy to find in an email dashboard. In common Klaviyo email reporting, recorded unique opens are compared with successfully delivered emails. But a recorded open is not the same as confirmed human attention.

Apple Mail Privacy Protection changes what an open means

Apple Mail Privacy Protection (MPP) can preload tracking pixels, creating recorded opens without confirming that a person read the message. Klaviyo documents this behavior and reporting methods for affected opens. 2

Therefore an apparent increase in open rate may not represent a similar increase in readership. Changes in your email client mix or tracking setup can also affect comparability.

Better reporting note: “Recorded opens increased. Apple Mail privacy activity may affect comparability, so we will review clicks and downstream actions before changing strategy.”

Use open rate as a limited diagnostic signal—not a reliable stand-alone verdict on subject-line quality or campaign effectiveness.

Seven ecommerce email metrics worth tracking

1. Delivery rate: are your emails being accepted?

Delivery rate is the proportion of sent messages reported as successfully delivered. A simplified formula is delivered emails ÷ sent emails × 100.

Successful delivery generally means the receiving server accepted the message. It does not guarantee inbox placement or readership. Review bounces and other deliverability diagnostics alongside it. Ask whether a change in acquisition sources, recipient list, mailbox providers, domain authentication or sending patterns might explain a deterioration. 6

2. Click rate: are recipients taking a next step?

Click rate compares unique recorded clicking recipients with delivered messages: unique clicking recipients ÷ delivered emails × 100.

Klaviyo distinguishes click rate from click-to-open rate. The latter divides clicks by recorded opens and can inherit the limitations of open tracking. Click rate can help show whether the email directed interest to a relevant destination, but it still needs interpretation.

Not every recorded click is a human click

Email security software can visit links automatically to check safety. Klaviyo identifies certain events as bot clicks and offers settings to exclude identified bot activity from some reporting and attribution calculations. 3

Before comparing campaigns, confirm whether bot-click filtering was applied consistently. Review which links received clicks, the segment, destination page and actual customer actions.

3. Conversion rate: did a meaningful action occur?

A typical ecommerce conversion metric is Placed Order, but reports may be configured around different tracked events. A simplified attributed conversion rate is unique attributed conversions ÷ successfully delivered emails × 100, subject to the platform's metric definitions.

Always state the selected conversion event. A welcome message may focus on first orders, a product education email on a specific product action, and post-purchase communication on another relevant outcome. Comparing metrics with different event definitions can mislead. 4

4. Revenue per recipient: how much revenue was attributed relative to reach?

For email campaign reporting, revenue per recipient often means attributed revenue ÷ delivered emails. It puts attributed revenue into context when campaigns have different audience sizes.

It is not the same as profit per recipient. It does not automatically subtract product costs, fulfillment, discounts or purchases that would have happened anyway. Use it as an attribution-based measure, not a universal profitability score.

5. Unsubscribe rate: are recipients opting out?

Unsubscribe rate shows the proportion of recipients who unsubscribed relative to the platform's defined delivered-message denominator. An increase might warrant checking targeting, expectations, cadence, messaging and recent list-source changes.

But an unsubscribe does not tell you exactly why an individual left. Examine the trend and circumstances before changing the whole program.

6. Spam complaint rate: are recipients reporting unwanted messages?

Spam complaints are reported when recipients mark messages as spam through relevant mailbox interfaces. This is not the same thing as an email landing in a spam folder.

Monitor complaints alongside bounces and delivery data. If complaints climb, investigate acquisition sources, consent records, audience fit, recent changes and message relevance. 6

7. Repeat purchase behavior: what happens after the first order?

Retention work extends beyond the initial conversion. Useful store-level questions include how many customers order again, the interval between purchases, and how behavior differs across meaningful customer groups.

These are customer and business metrics rather than necessarily native campaign-level metrics. A consumables brand may consider replenishment cycles; an infrequent-purchase category may need a longer horizon. Compare customer cohorts carefully and avoid claiming that email caused all observed repeat orders.

A simple ecommerce email metrics scorecard

A weekly report does not need dozens of numbers. It needs to describe what was sent, to whom, what was observed and which decision follows.

MetricQuestion it answers
Sends and deliveriesHow much eligible audience was reached?
Bounces and complaintsAre there warning signs in sending quality?
Click rateDid people interact with a relevant destination?
Attributed conversionsWhich selected customer events received credit?
Revenue per recipientHow much revenue was credited relative to delivered volume?
UnsubscribesDid people signal they no longer wanted messages?
Customer/cohort outcomeWhat happened to first and repeat orders?
Change notesWhat changed that could affect interpretation?

That last row is important. A scorecard without context shows movement but does not tell you why it happened. Record meaningful changes to audience, product selection, discounts, website experience, attribution settings and send timing.

Worked example: reading a campaign report without overclaiming

Consider a hypothetical ecommerce campaign. The figures below are educational illustrations, not Proper Emails client data or a benchmark.

Illustrative metricValue
Successfully delivered emails10,000
Unique recorded clicking recipients150
Attributed orders30
Attributed order revenue$3,000
Unsubscribes25

Click rate: 150 ÷ 10,000 × 100 = 1.5%.

Attributed order rate: 30 ÷ 10,000 × 100 = 0.3%.

Attributed revenue per delivered email: $3,000 ÷ 10,000 = $0.30.

Unsubscribe rate: 25 ÷ 10,000 × 100 = 0.25%.

These calculations describe the hypothetical report, but they do not establish that this campaign performed well or badly. First ask whether clicks include automated activity, what attribution window was in effect, which customers received the message, whether the landing page worked and how comparable campaigns were measured.

Most importantly, $3,000 in attributed revenue does not prove that the campaign generated $3,000 in additional sales.

Email attribution vs. incrementality: know the difference

Attribution asks: which interaction receives credit?

Attribution assigns a conversion to one or more marketing touchpoints according to configured rules. In Klaviyo, attribution depends on settings such as eligible interactions and the lookback window. An order can receive email credit if qualifying activity occurs within the configured period before a purchase. 4

Those rules organize reporting, but they do not establish that the email caused the order.

Incrementality asks: what changed because of the email?

Some customers might have ordered without the campaign. Others might have ordered because of it. A typical attribution report does not reliably distinguish those cases.

Estimating incremental impact generally requires an appropriate experimental approach, such as a randomized holdout or control group, built around a clear decision and supported by sufficient data.

The distinction: Attribution assigns credit. Incrementality attempts to estimate causal impact. They are different questions.

How to review campaigns and automated flows differently

Campaign reporting

A scheduled campaign targets a selected audience for a launch, announcement, offer or educational message. Review that audience, the email's stated purpose, deliveries, unique clicks, relevant attributed outcomes and negative signals.

Compare similar campaigns when possible. A product launch sent to a highly engaged segment is not automatically comparable with a general educational message sent to a broader list.

Flow reporting

Automated flows depend on event triggers, filters and sending rules. In addition to message metrics, ask whether eligible profiles entered, why some people were skipped, whether the intended event occurred and whether purchase or progression filters prevented irrelevant follow-ups.

Klaviyo's flow analytics includes recipient activity, skipped-message reasons, link activity and selected conversion metrics, which can help inspect these operational questions. 5

Low delivered volume may reflect a small eligible audience rather than weak creative. Start with the flow's trigger and purpose. For a related lifecycle distinction, read Cart abandonment vs. checkout abandonment.

A better weekly email reporting routine

Step 1: Confirm the reporting period

Choose a consistent date range, and be aware that recent conversions may be incomplete while the attribution window remains open.

Step 2: Verify metric definitions

Record the selected conversion event, attribution settings, bot-click filtering and Apple Privacy Open handling. If definitions change, label the break in comparability.

Step 3: Separate campaigns from flows

Compare like with like. Use campaign reports for campaign decisions and flow reports for automation decisions.

Step 4: Identify a meaningful change

For instance, unique clicks may fall while delivery volume remains broadly stable. That is a reason to inspect the email and customer journey—not proof that one change caused the decline.

Step 5: Follow the customer journey

Open the actual message, review the subject and CTA, and test its destination on mobile. The report suggests where to investigate; the journey shows what a real recipient sees.

Step 6: Choose one testable hypothesis

Instead of “increase engagement,” write: “Some subscribers may be uncertain about the difference between our two product sizes. We will test whether a clearer comparison improves relevant product-page visits.” Define the audience, outcome and method before starting.

Step 7: Record the decision

End each report with three things: what we observed, what remains uncertain and what we will investigate or test next.

Common email reporting mistakes

1. Treating an open as confirmed readership

Apple Mail Privacy Protection can generate recorded opens without a person actually reading the message.

2. Assuming every click came from a person

Some security systems follow links automatically. Check the platform's bot-click definitions and filtering options.

3. Treating attributed revenue as incremental profit

Attribution is not proof of causality and does not automatically include costs and margins.

4. Comparing unrelated audiences

An established engaged segment and a newly acquired list can differ for legitimate reasons. Add the audience context before making a comparison.

5. Changing too many variables at once

When offer, timing, audience and creative all change, it becomes difficult to isolate which decision mattered.

6. Reporting numbers without an action

A weekly report is most valuable when it supports a specific next decision.

Frequently asked questions

What are the most important ecommerce email marketing metrics?

Start with deliveries, unique click rate, meaningful conversion events, attributed revenue per recipient, unsubscribes and spam complaints. For retention, add customer-level measures such as repeat purchase behavior. Choose metrics based on the email's purpose.

Are email open rates still useful?

They can offer limited context, but privacy-related tracking effects mean they should not be treated as a precise measure of human readership.

What is a good ecommerce email click rate?

There is no universal target appropriate to every brand, audience and message. Compare relevant messages using consistent definitions and suitable benchmarks.

What is revenue per recipient?

It is attributed revenue divided by the relevant recipient or delivered-message count, as defined by the reporting platform. It does not automatically represent incremental profit.

Does email-attributed revenue mean email caused those purchases?

No. Attribution applies credit using a selected model. Estimating causal impact requires a suitable measurement approach.

Should I measure campaigns and flows separately?

Yes. Their audiences, timing and purposes often differ. Separate reviews usually lead to clearer decisions.

Final takeaway: report what helps you decide

You do not need a dashboard full of impressive numbers to manage a better ecommerce email program. You need to know what each metric measures, how reliable the signal is, and what decision it supports.

Start with deliverability, then look at clicks and relevant customer actions. Interpret attributed revenue carefully. Monitor unsubscribes and complaints. Connect your messages to customer behavior over time.

The most useful report does not end with “performance increased.” It ends with a clearer explanation of what happened and a concrete next action.
